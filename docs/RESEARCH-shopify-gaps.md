# Where Shopify falls short for very small merchants — research brief

**Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Question:** where does Shopify fall short for solo
makers, service businesses that also sell goods, workshop and class hosts,
and local shops — and what would a new small-business commerce platform need
to be a credible alternative? Sources are web search results read on this
date; figures are as the cited pages state them and were not independently
verified against vendor invoices. Anything marked *unverified* rests on one
secondary source.

## 1. What Shopify costs a small merchant

Shopify's paid plans are Basic at $39/month, Grow at $105/month and Advanced
at $399/month, or about $29, $79 and $299 on annual billing
(https://sherocommerce.com/blogs/insights/shopify-pricing;
https://shopexperts.com/help/pricing/shopify-plans-pricing). Card processing
through Shopify Payments is 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic, 2.7% + 30¢ on Grow and
2.5% + 30¢ on Advanced (https://commerce-ui.com/insights/shopify-pricing).
Using any other payment provider adds a Shopify surcharge on top of the
provider's own fee: 2% on Basic, 1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced
(https://ecomm.design/shopify-pricing-guide/).

The plan is the floor, not the bill. A small store typically adds $10–$100 a
month of apps for email, reviews and SEO; mid-market stores spend $350–$1,400
a month on apps (https://commerce-ui.com/insights/shopify-pricing). Apps
increasingly charge a monthly fee plus usage-based pricing (a share of sales,
per click, per action), which merchants on Shopify's own forum describe as
worse than a partnership demand, with one thread titled "Apps have become
worse than the mafia"
(https://community.shopify.com/t/apps-have-become-worse-than-the-mafia/388157).
Merchants note that apps charge for behaviour Shopify Flow could do for free,
but Flow is too complex for an average owner to use
(https://community.shopify.com/t/how-much-does-it-cost-to-run-an-online-store-per-month/394576/1).
App reviews record small boutiques being told to move to Shopify Plus
($2,000+/month) to unlock features
(https://apps.shopify.com/reviews/1340892).

A realistic all-in for a maker on Basic with a booking app, a reviews app and
email is therefore in the region of $80–$150 a month before processing fees,
and processing on a third-party gateway costs the gateway's fee plus
Shopify's 2%. *unverified as a single figure; composed from the pages above.*

## 2. Why small merchants churn or never start

**App-stack cost and drift.** See above: the platform is priced as a floor
and the working store is assembled from apps whose prices move independently
(https://community.shopify.com/t/apps-have-become-worse-than-the-mafia/388157).

**Complexity.** Shopify is positioned, even by its advocates, as the choice
for sellers who need scalability, multi-channel and automation, while
Squarespace is recommended for creators, small boutiques and service-led
businesses that want simplicity and a lower all-in cost for a modest
catalogue (https://squarespace-expert.com/blog/squarespace-vs-shopify-2025-for-small-business/).
Merchants asking how to leave cite templates that are too restrictive
(https://community.shopify.com/t/i-no-longer-want-to-have-my-website-on-shopify-how-can-i-integrate-it-into-square-space/67740).

**No services or bookings in the box.** Shopify has no native appointment
calendar, staff schedule or time-slot picker; a merchant who sells a class or
a workshop installs an app
(https://www.logbase.io/blog/add-appointments-with-shopify-booking-app;
https://www.shopify.com/blog/appointment-booking-app). The booking apps push
group classes, a second staff member and multi-day sessions into higher
tiers, and per-booking pricing scales with volume
(https://cowlendar.com/blog/best-shopify-appointment-booking-apps-for-service-businesses-(2026);
https://opencals.com/blog/best-shopify-appointment-apps). A workshop or
ticketed-experience business is told to look for an event-focused solution
beyond the appointment apps
(https://www2.eveyevents.com/blog/can-you-book-appointments-on-shopify/). No
2026 announcement of native bookings was found (search of Shopify Editions
coverage, 2026-09-03; *unverified absence*).

**Payments in India.** Shopify Payments is not available in India; every
Indian store integrates Razorpay, PayU, CCAvenue or Cashfree
(https://web.skypilotapp.com/blogs/wiki/is-shopify-payments-available-in-india;
https://www.doola.com/blog/how-to-set-up-payment-gateways-for-your-shopify-store-in-india/).
Razorpay charges about 2% on domestic cards, UPI and net banking, and
Shopify adds its own third-party surcharge on top
(https://projectsupply.in/blog/shopify-payment-gateway-india-razorpay-payu-ccavenue).
An Indian merchant on Basic therefore pays roughly 4% per order plus $39 a
month, against local builders that charge a flat rupee fee per order.

## 3. How competitors position and what they charge

- **Square Online**: free plan at 3.3% + 30¢ per online transaction; Plus at
  $29/month with lower processing; Premium $79–149/month
  (https://findstack.com/compare/square-online-vs-wix-ecommerce). Sells on
  the strength of the card reader and POS already in the shop.
- **Wix**: Core plan $29/month with Wix Bookings included at no extra charge;
  Bookings supports one-to-one appointments, group classes, multi-session
  courses and packages inside the same editor and dashboard as the store
  (https://ecommerceparadise.com/best-ecommerce-platform-for-services-in-2026-top-6-for-selling-services-online/;
  https://www.techradar.com/pro/website-building/squarespace-acuity-review-year).
- **Squarespace**: Core at $29/month with 0% transaction fees; scheduling is
  Acuity, a connected but separate product at $16, $27 or $49 a month on top
  (https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/website-builders/squarespace-pricing/;
  https://www.tooltester.com/en/reviews/squarespace-review/pricing/).
- **Big Cartel**: five products free, paid tiers by product count, aimed at
  artists (https://www.ecommerce-gold.com/big-cartel-vs-squarespace/).
- **Ecwid (Lightspeed)**: free to ten products, $25–$85/month, no transaction
  fees, embeds into an existing site (https://www.ecommerce-gold.com/ecwid-vs-wix/).
- **WooCommerce**: free software on self-hosted WordPress; cost is hosting,
  plugins and the owner's time (general knowledge; *unverified figure*).
- **Etsy**: $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee on the order including
  shipping, plus payment processing of 3% + $0.25 for US sellers; about $2.80
  on a $20 item with $5 shipping (https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees).
- **Gumroad**: no monthly fee, 10% + $0.50 per direct sale, 30% on
  marketplace sales, card processing charged separately
  (https://www.swell.is/content/gumroad-pricing).
- **SumUp**: no monthly fee, 2.5% per online payment, free online store with
  the card reader (https://www.expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/sumup-fees-costs).
- **Dukaan (India)**: free plan at 4.99% + gateway fees per order, ₹299/month
  at 3.99%, ₹999/month at 1.99%, commission capped at ₹200 per order, free
  plan capped at ₹1,00,000 of UPI volume (https://www.techjockey.com/detail/dukaan).
- **Instamojo (India)**: free plan with higher transaction fees; premium about
  ₹999/month or ₹6,999–14,999 a year (https://saasrat.com/products/instamojo-ecommerce).
- **Shoopy (India)**: ₹0 / ₹2,799 / ₹5,999 / ₹13,999 a year, then a flat ₹2–3
  per order after a threshold of 50–1,000 orders per cycle
  (https://shoopy.in/dukaan-vs-shoopy).

The pattern: everyone below Shopify competes on one of three things —
no monthly fee (Square, SumUp, Gumroad), bookings in the box (Wix, and
Squarespace via Acuity), or a flat per-order rupee fee instead of a
percentage (Shoopy). Nobody found competes on all three, and nobody found
puts bookable classes and physical goods in one catalogue with one checkout
and one customer record as the default shape of a shop.

## 4. Shopify's AI: what helps and what does not

Shopify Magic generates product descriptions, edits images and drafts
replies; Sidekick is the conversational assistant in the admin. Shopify
reports 385% year-on-year growth in shops actively using Sidekick and about
100 million conversations by late 2025
(https://www.adsx.com/blog/shopify-magic-sidekick-ai-features-2026).
Merchants praise description drafting and inbox reply suggestions, and some
report 10–20% higher average order value from AI recommendations
(https://pagefly.io/blogs/shopify/shopify-sidekick;
https://dodropshipping.com/shopify-magic-review/). The recurring complaint is
that both tools hallucinate product attributes when metadata is sparse and
give imprecise answers on complex tasks, so output is a first draft to be
checked, not a result (https://www.firstpier.com/glossary/shopify-magic;
https://ecommercefastlane.com/shopify-magic-vs-shopify-sidekick/). Sidekick
now recommends apps from inside the admin
(https://community.shopify.dev/t/sidekick-gets-smarter-app-recommendations/22753),
which points the AI at the app store rather than away from it.

What no source shows Shopify's AI doing: setting up a shop from a
description of the business, changing the shop's data model, or running the
order desk. Its AI writes copy inside a structure the merchant still has to
build and pay for.

## 5. Goods and bookable services in one shop, today

Wix is the closest: Bookings is native, included from $29/month, and covers
appointments, group classes, courses and packages beside a product catalogue
(https://ecommerceparadise.com/best-ecommerce-platform-for-services-in-2026-top-6-for-selling-services-online/).
Squarespace bolts Acuity on for $16–49 a month more and keeps it a separate
product (https://www.techradar.com/pro/website-building/squarespace-acuity-review-year).
GoDaddy includes bookings from $14.99 (same source). Shopify does it only
through apps whose tiers price group classes and a second staff member as
upgrades (section 2). Square splits the job across Square Online and Square
Appointments, two products on one account (*unverified as to combined
pricing*).

None of the sources describe a platform where a booking and a product share
one cart, one order history, one customer and one seller queue. Wix comes
nearest and still treats bookings as a module beside the store.

## 6. What a credible alternative must have

Ranked, each tied to the evidence above.

1. **One price a maker can predict**: a flat monthly fee or a flat per-order
   fee, no percentage on top of the gateway, because the percentage-plus-
   surcharge model is the single most-cited grievance (sections 1, 2, 3).
2. **Bookings and goods in one catalogue, cart and order queue by default**,
   because Shopify has none, Wix has a module, and nobody has the unified
   shape (sections 2, 5).
3. **Group classes, multi-session courses and a second staff member on the
   base plan**, because those are exactly what the booking apps gate behind
   tiers (section 2).
4. **Local payment rails first in the launch market**: UPI, Razorpay or
   Cashfree in India with no platform surcharge, because Shopify Payments is
   absent there and the surcharge doubles the fee (section 2).
5. **No app store as a dependency for the basics**: reviews, email receipts,
   discounts and a booking calendar must ship in the box, because the app
   stack is where Shopify's cost and complexity actually live (section 1).
6. **An AI that builds and runs the shop, not one that writes copy inside
   it**: setting up from a description, proposing the data model, answering
   the order desk — the gap Shopify's Magic and Sidekick leave (section 4).
7. **A store a customer can find**: crawlable pages, sitemaps and structured
   data on day one, because a solo maker has no marketing budget and search
   is the free channel (Etsy's pull is discovery, section 3).
8. **Guest checkout and account-free ordering** to match the friction level
   of Gumroad and Square, where a buyer never registers (section 3).
9. **A free tier bounded by volume, not by features**, following Square,
   Ecwid and Dukaan, so a maker's first ten sales cost nothing (section 3).
10. **Honest AI output**: generated copy visibly marked as a draft and never
    inventing specifications, because hallucinated attributes are the one
    consistent complaint about Shopify's AI (section 4).
